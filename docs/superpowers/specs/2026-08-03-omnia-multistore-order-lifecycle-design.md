## Executive Outcome

Omnia becomes the single operating screen for UAE, KSA, WhatsApp, and WooCommerce orders. A clean order should move from store ingestion to payment verification, warehouse allocation, inventory reservation, invoice and label generation, dispatch, delivery, cancellation, refund, or exchange without Excel or routine portal-hopping.

The target is not “remove every person.” The target is to remove repetitive coordination and make one trained operator capable of supervising all stores and warehouses while physical staff continue picking and packing. Humans work an exception queue; software handles the normal path.

```text
Shopify UAE/KSA/WA + WooCommerce
                │
                ▼
      Canonical order lifecycle
                │
      ┌─────────┼──────────┐
      ▼         ▼          ▼
   payment   inventory   warehouse
   evidence  reservation allocation
      │         │          │
      └─────────┼──────────┘
                ▼
       invoice → label → courier
                │
                ▼
      delivery / cancel / refund / exchange
                │
                ▼
       Zoho + stores + audit + notifications
```

## Problem Statement

Operations are already partially digitized, but people still bridge systems manually:

- A UAE operator checks Stripe or Tamara, confirms payment, initiates invoicing, and tells inventory and packing teams when to begin.
- Yaseen checks KSA availability and payment, builds an Excel SKU sheet, and sends it to SMSA KSA for fulfillment.
- Two inventory staff pick items and three packing staff pack them.
- A labeling role generates shipping labels and RF codes.
- UAE SMSA collection happens around 3 PM; Ontrack collection happens around 9 PM.
- Two finance staff reconcile and account for activity.
- A manager coordinates the overall process and closes the sales day.
- Marketing, creative production, product listing, and WhatsApp support are adjacent teams but are not part of the first OMS automation boundary.

The codebase already contains order fetching, inventory webhooks, WooCommerce APIs, provider credentials, Zoho integration, reconciliation, reservations, invoice generation, SMSA shipment creation, and partial order-event history. The missing product is a trustworthy lifecycle core that makes these pieces behave as one system.

## Demand Evidence

Fouad expects automation substantial enough that one operator can manage all operational workflows and warehouses. The current business pays for repeated checking, copying, messaging, and spreadsheet preparation even though most source data and integrations already exist.

The strongest evidence is the existing human workflow: employees continuously check payment portals, prepare Excel sheets, coordinate physical teams, create labels, and reconcile finance. This is recurring operating cost, not hypothetical demand.

## Target User and Narrowest Wedge

The primary user is the Omnia system operator replacing the UAE/KSA administrative coordination layer.

The narrowest valuable wedge is:

> Every paid, fulfillable order from all four stores automatically reaches “ready to pick” with verified payment evidence, an assigned warehouse, reserved inventory, generated documents, and a visible exception if any prerequisite fails.

That wedge eliminates payment-portal checking and Excel preparation while proving the inventory foundation needed for safe dispatch, cancellation, refund, and exchange automation.

## Non-Destructive Constraints

1. Do not drop, truncate, replace, or destructively rename existing tables or columns.
2. Preserve existing orders, reconciliation history, inventory data, invoices, attachments, Zoho records, and webhook data.
3. Add lifecycle capability beside the current system, using existing `orders.uid` plus `tenant_id` as references.
4. Backfills must be repeatable, checkpointed, count-verified, and safe to rerun.
5. Inventory history is append-only. Corrections use compensating movements rather than editing prior movements.
6. Automation begins in shadow mode and activates by tenant, store, and command type.
7. Existing UI and APIs continue to work during migration.
8. Disabling lifecycle workers must restore the previous operating path without data restoration.

## Agreed Premises

1. Zoho remains the inventory/accounting authority; Omnia orchestrates and audits changes.
2. The first rollout automates order operations and finance coordination, not campaign management or creative production.
3. Inventory, refunds, cancellations, and exchanges must be transactional, idempotent, and reversible.
4. Staffing changes follow a production proof window, not merely code completion.
5. The target team is one system operator plus the physical picking and packing capacity required by order volume.
6. At least 95% of clean orders should flow without administrative intervention, while 100% of mutations have durable audit records.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Safe Incremental Automation — Chosen

Keep every store in scope, but activate lifecycle commands progressively: observe, shadow, supervised live, then automatic. Reuse existing connectors and add one canonical command/event boundary.

- Effort: 8–12 focused engineering days for the clean-path wedge; 15–20 for the full after-sales lifecycle.
- Operational proof: 20 business days of progressively live traffic.
- Risk: Low to medium, controlled through flags, idempotency, reconciliation, and compensating actions.

### Approach B: Operator Cockpit First

Finish portal screens quickly while keeping payment and inventory actions partially manual.

- Effort: 3–4 days for visible workflow consolidation.
- Benefit: removes some Excel and portal switching.
- Limitation: does not justify meaningful staffing reduction because the operator still performs the underlying checks.

### Approach C: Full Cutover

Enable automated payment, stock, dispatch, refunds, and exchanges together after implementation.

- Effort: potentially 7–9 engineering days.
- Risk: high. A hidden idempotency or provider-edge-case bug could create duplicate shipments, incorrect stock, or duplicate refunds across every store.

## Recommended Architecture

### Definitions and metric boundary

- **Eligible order:** a supported-store order with normalized line items/SKUs, a routable address, a supported payment method, and no explicit fraud/manual-review hold.
- **Clean order:** an eligible order for which payment evidence agrees with the store total, one warehouse can cover all items, and the courier accepts the address/service.
- **Clean administrative path:** ingestion through ready-to-pick without a person checking a provider portal, editing stock, producing an Excel sheet, or creating documents manually.
- **Clean-path automatic-through rate:** clean orders reaching the phase target automatically divided by all clean orders observed in the same window. Provider outages remain in this metric when they block an otherwise clean order; deliberately unsupported policies are excluded and reported with counts.
- **Eligible-order completion rate:** eligible orders reaching either the phase target or an explicit business exception divided by all eligible orders. This measures whether the system loses work, not whether the business inputs were clean.
- **Controllable automation exception:** a software, integration, stale-data, or rule failure that should have been automated. Genuine shortage, invalid customer address, fraud hold, or provider decline is a business exception and is measured separately.
- **Payment evidence:** a provider transaction/reference whose amount, currency, status, tenant/store merchant account, and order reference satisfy a provider-specific rule.
- **Accepted movement:** an immutable inventory movement committed by the database transaction; provider/store synchronization may still be pending.

### Canonical lifecycle

Use separate dimensions instead of one overloaded status:

- Order: `created | confirmed | cancelled | closed`
- Payment: `pending | authorized | paid | partially_refunded | refunded | failed`
- Fulfillment: `unallocated | allocated | picking | packed | labeled | dispatched | in_transit | delivered | returned`
- Inventory: `unreserved | reserved | committed | released | restocked | quarantined`
- After-sale case: `requested | approved | collecting | received | inspected | completed | rejected`

The visible order summary may derive a single headline state, but source dimensions remain independent. A delivered order can later become partially refunded; a cancelled paid order requires both refund and stock release.

Headline precedence is deterministic: active exception/uncertain outcome → after-sale case → cancellation requested → fulfillment state → payment state → created. Examples: `delivered + partially_refunded` displays “Partially refunded”; `dispatched + cancellation requested` displays “Intercept/return required,” never “Cancelled.”

Legal transitions are allowlisted. Commands that violate a guard are rejected and logged:

| Action | Guard | Inventory consequence | Financial consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirm payment | provider evidence valid or authorized override | none | payment becomes paid |
| Reserve | paid/COD eligible; warehouse covers every item | available → reserved | none |
| Dispatch | reservation exists; label/AWB confirmed | reserved → committed | COD receivable may open |
| Cancel pre-reservation | not dispatched | none | refund if money captured |
| Cancel reserved | not dispatched | reserved → available | refund if money captured |
| Cancel dispatched | shipment exists | no immediate restock | intercept/return case; refund follows policy |
| Receive return | tracked returned units physically received | returned units → quarantine | refund may remain pending inspection |
| Pass inspection | inspected quantity confirmed | quarantine → available | refund/exchange may complete |
| Fail inspection | inspected quantity confirmed | quarantine → damaged | operator resolves refund policy |
| Exchange outbound | replacement approved and incoming policy satisfied | standard reserve/dispatch movements | price difference charged/refunded separately |

Additional transitions: payment failure keeps fulfillment unreserved; later valid payment evidence may recover it. Partial fulfillment creates item/quantity-scoped reservation and shipment legs and cannot mark the whole order delivered until every leg is terminal. Failed delivery opens a return-to-origin case without increasing available stock. Case rejection records reason and closes without financial/stock compensation unless an explicit override command is approved.

### Durable command boundary

All manual actions and webhooks submit lifecycle commands such as:

- `confirm_payment`
- `allocate_warehouse`
- `reserve_inventory`
- `generate_invoice`
- `create_shipment`
- `cancel_order`
- `refund_order`
- `request_exchange`
- `receive_return`
- `complete_exchange`

Each command carries `tenant_id`, `order_uid`, actor, source, idempotency key, requested time, and payload. A uniqueness constraint prevents the same webhook or button retry from applying twice.

Command state is `received | processing | succeeded | rejected | failed_retryable | uncertain_external | compensated`. Repeating the same key and payload returns the stored result. Reusing a key with a different payload is rejected. Commands are never expired merely to permit replay; an explicit new command/key is required. Row locking or serializable database functions protect concurrent commands for the same order/stock balance.

External effects are at-least-once, not magically exactly-once. When a provider may have accepted a request but the response was lost, the command enters `uncertain_external`; the system polls/searches by provider idempotency/reference before retrying. A human resolves only when provider reconciliation cannot determine the outcome.

### Additive data model

Existing tables remain untouched except nullable compatibility references/indexes where required. Proposed additions:

- `lifecycle_commands`: tenant/order, command type, canonical payload hash, idempotency key, state, result/error, actor/source/correlation, timestamps. Unique `(tenant_id, idempotency_key)`; index `(tenant_id, order_uid, requested_at)`.
- `order_lifecycle_events`: immutable transition/event facts referencing the command. Unique source-event identity; index `(tenant_id, order_uid, occurred_at)`.
- `inventory_movements`: immutable SKU/warehouse/location deltas by stock bucket, reason, order, command, and movement group. Unique `(tenant_id, command_id, sku, warehouse_id, bucket, sequence)`.
- `provider_attempts`: provider operation, request fingerprint, provider idempotency/reference, attempt state, sanitized response/error, retry time. Unique provider reference where available.
- `notification_outbox`: event, audience/channel/template, delivery state, attempts, next attempt, dedup key. Unique `(tenant_id, dedup_key)`.
- `after_sale_cases` and `after_sale_lines`: cancellation/return/refund/exchange case, original and replacement items, quantities, inspection disposition, financial references, and state.
- `automation_decisions`: shadow proposal, observed human decision, agreement category, reviewer, resolution, and promotion eligibility.

Implementation migrations must turn these concepts into typed, non-null-by-default columns, tenant-composite foreign keys, explicit delete restrictions, monotonic per-order event versions, and projection rebuild functions. `source_event_identity` is `<provider>:<store>:<delivery/event id>`; `movement_group` is the lifecycle command UUID shared by balanced legs; agreement categories are `agree | human_override | automation_error | policy_gap | source_data_gap`. Tenant SLA and stock tolerance are tenant configuration, not constants.

All foreign keys include or validate tenant ownership. Critical command acceptance, lifecycle event, inventory movement, and outbox intent commit in one database transaction. Provider calls happen after commit through the outbox/attempt worker. Audit retention is indefinite for financial/inventory facts; raw provider payload retention is configurable and redacted.

### Authority and degraded mode

Zoho remains the accounting/on-hand authority; Omnia owns reservations and orchestration. Available-to-promise is derived from the last accepted Zoho quantity minus active Omnia reservations. If Zoho is stale beyond the tenant SLA or disagrees beyond tolerance, automatic reservation pauses and orders enter an inventory-authority exception. Omnia never invents availability while Zoho is unavailable.

### Transactional inventory

Every quantity change creates one durable stock movement tied to an order and lifecycle command:

```text
reservation       available -N, reserved +N
dispatch          reserved -N, committed +N
cancel pre-ship   reserved -N, available +N
return received   quarantine +N
inspection pass   quarantine -N, available +N
inspection fail   quarantine -N, damaged +N
exchange outbound available/reserved -N through normal fulfillment
```

Movement groups must balance within the inventory model: reservation transfers `available -N` and `reserved +N`; dispatch transfers `reserved -N` and `committed +N`; release transfers `reserved -N` and `available +N`. A physical return introduces `quarantine +N` paired with an explicit external-return receipt leg, not an unexplained increase; inspection transfers quarantine to available or damaged. Per tenant/SKU/warehouse, projected balances must equal opening balance plus external receipts/adjustments and all balanced internal transfers.

Store and Zoho synchronization becomes a consequence of an accepted stock movement, not separate business logic in each webhook route. Provider writes have their own attempt records and retry independently without replaying the underlying movement.

### Cancellation, refund, and exchange

- Cancellation before reservation: close the order; no stock movement.
- Cancellation after reservation but before dispatch: release reservation and update every channel.
- Cancellation after dispatch: open a return/intercept case; never pretend stock is already available.
- Refund: store amount, currency, gateway reference, line allocation, reason, and provider status; support partial and full refunds.
- Exchange: model the returned item and replacement item as linked legs. Incoming stock remains quarantined until inspected; outgoing replacement uses the standard reservation/dispatch flow.
- Every action appears in the order history and finance reconciliation, including failed attempts and manual overrides.

### Notification outbox

Lifecycle transactions write notification intents in the same database transaction. A worker delivers Telegram, email, and in-app notifications and records attempts.

Create notification intents for the following; delivery policy decides immediate versus digest:

- inventory is reserved, released, committed, restocked, quarantined, or corrected;
- a provider sync fails or remains delayed beyond its SLA;
- payment evidence is missing or contradictory;
- an order cannot be allocated from one warehouse;
- a refund/exchange requires approval or fails;
- courier cutoff is approaching with unshipped orders;
- any automatic action is overridden manually.

Routine successes are summarized; exceptions alert immediately. Otherwise notification volume becomes another manual workload.

Delivery policy: exceptions, uncertain provider outcomes, cutoff risk, and manual overrides alert immediately; successful per-unit movements appear in-app and in a digest; normal order-stage successes remain silent unless customer communication is required.

### Multi-tenant boundary

Every new lifecycle, command, movement, notification, and after-sale record includes `tenant_id`. Unique keys and queries include tenant scope. Provider credentials and feature flags resolve by tenant/store; no new code may depend on `DEFAULT_TENANT_ID` or a hard-coded actor.

Roles are explicit: picker/packer may confirm assigned physical tasks; operator may resolve routine exceptions; finance may approve/refund within configured thresholds; manager/admin may override stock or high-value refunds. Sensitive overrides require reason and, above tenant thresholds, second approval. Audit rows are immutable to application roles; PII and provider payloads are minimized/redacted; secret rotation is verified before live activation.

### Recovery and operations

- Dead-letter queues retain exhausted provider/notification attempts with replay and operator-resolution tools.
- Reconciliation jobs compare lifecycle projections, movement balances, provider status, Zoho, and storefront quantities.
- Kill switches exist per tenant, store, provider, and command type.
- Degraded mode preserves ingestion and queues commands while pausing unsafe external mutations.
- Recovery targets: no accepted inventory/financial command loss; restore processing from durable queues after service recovery; exact RTO/RPO documented from hosting/database capabilities before activation.

### Interaction-state contract

| State | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Loading/long-running | Show command ID, current phase, elapsed time, and safe navigation; never invite a duplicate submission |
| Empty | Distinguish “no work” from filters, stale sync, and missing permissions |
| Stale authority | Display watermark/age, disable unsafe automation, and offer refresh/escalation |
| Partial provider failure | Preserve successful facts, show failed provider leg and retry/reconcile action |
| Confirmation | Explain inventory/financial consequence; high-risk actions require reason and threshold approval |
| Compensation | No generic undo; offer an explicit compensating command and preview its effects |
| Bulk action | Only homogeneous, individually validated commands; show excluded rows and per-order results |
| Permission denied | Name required role/approval path without exposing sensitive data |
| Responsive/accessibility | Operator desktop/tablet first; keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic status text, and non-color-only alerts |

## Success Criteria and Workforce Gate

### Technical acceptance

- All stores ingest orders and lifecycle updates with tenant-scoped idempotency.
- Duplicate webhook and command replays create no duplicate stock movement, shipment, refund, or notification.
- Inventory reservations and compensating movements reconcile to zero unexplained variance.
- Full and partial cancellation/refund/exchange paths retain complete history.
- CRUD permissions and tenant isolation are tested.
- Provider failures retry without replaying accepted business mutations.
- Existing reconciliation, invoice, and order history remain intact.
- Provider “accepted but response lost” cases reconcile to a known result or remain visibly `uncertain_external`; they are never blindly retried.
- Role, approval-threshold, tenant-isolation, stale-authority, kill-switch, dead-letter replay, and degraded-mode paths are tested.

### Operational acceptance

- At least 95% of clean orders complete the clean administrative path without manual intervention.
- 100% of eligible orders reach the phase target or a visible, classified business/automation exception.
- 100% of inventory and financial mutations have durable audit history.
- No unexplained stock variance for 20 consecutive production business days.
- No duplicate shipment or duplicate refund.
- Controllable automation exceptions are below 5% of eligible orders and median resolution is under five minutes; business exceptions are reported separately.
- All orders eligible for courier collection are ready before the relevant cutoff, excluding documented customer/provider exceptions.

The initial wedge gate measures ingestion through ready-to-pick for all stores. Dispatch, refunds/cancellations, and exchanges each have separate promotion gates; wedge success does not imply after-sales readiness.

- **Dispatch promotion:** 50 supervised shipments across applicable warehouses/couriers, zero duplicate AWBs, provider status reconciled, and 100% accepted orders ready before cutoff excluding classified business exceptions.
- **Cancellation/refund promotion:** at least 20 representative cases including partial/full and uncertain-response simulations, zero duplicate refunds, amounts reconcile to provider/ledger, and stock compensation matches physical state.
- **Return/exchange promotion:** at least 10 end-to-end cases covering inspection pass/fail and price differences, every unit traceable through quarantine/disposition, and no replacement dispatch without a valid reservation.

## Migration and Backfill Verification

1. Record preflight row counts, per-tenant order counts, current status distributions, inventory totals, and checksums of protected reconciliation fields.
2. Apply additive DDL only; block destructive statements in review/CI.
3. Backfill in tenant/time-window batches with a checkpoint row after every batch. Derive events/projections without changing existing source fields.
4. After each batch, assert source count = processed + explicitly skipped, no cross-tenant references, monotonic event versions, no duplicate source identities, and zero unexplained inventory delta.
5. Shadow-read old and new projections and persist disagreements for review.
6. Rollback means disable lifecycle flags/workers and continue existing paths. Additive rows remain for diagnosis; no old data restoration is required.
7. Promote reads/writes per tenant/store only after invariant queries pass. Projection rebuild from immutable events must reproduce the promoted state before activation.

### Workforce gate
